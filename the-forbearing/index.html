She said, "Yes."

About the novel

In a year of drought a woman gave a stranger water from her last waterskin, and said to her son, "Water you hold back dies." Then she died. The boy carried the empty waterskin on his shoulder for sixty-eight years.

His name was Barhad, a herder's son from the desert. At eleven he walked two nights to warn a tribe that was not his own about a well gone bad. He gathered the copies of the Covenant from the tribes one by one, and carried them above his head through a night of flood. The desert named him the Forbearing, and then chose him Warden of the Covenant.

No one hated him but those who profit from fear.

Then came a man with a face for every door. He sat on the Warden's chair with the Warden's face. Everything people saw after that, they saw with their own eyes, and all of it was arranged.

Set more than a thousand years before Blue Ink. It can be read on its own.
